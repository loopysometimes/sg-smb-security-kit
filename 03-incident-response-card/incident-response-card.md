# What To Do If You Think You've Been Hacked
### A Step-By-Step Incident Response Card for Small Singapore Offices

*Keep this somewhere easy to find. If something goes wrong, this should be your first 60 minutes*

*This checklist was prepared as a practical guide for small  organisations. **It is not legal advice**. For specific PDPA compliance questions, consult the PDPC's advisory guidelines or a qualified data protection officer.*
---

## First: Don't Panic. Don't delete ANYTHING!

Your instinct will be to close everything, restart your computer, or 
delete suspicious files. Don't. You may accidentally destroy evidence 
that your IT vendor or the authorities need, destroying any files may possibly also implicate you.

**The two most important rules:**
1. Do not turn off or wipe the affected device
2. Do not stay silent. Tell someone immediately
---

## "I clicked a suspicious link"

**Do this in the next 10 minutes:**

- [ ] Disconnect the device from the internet immediately 
      (turn off WiFi, unplug the network cable)
- [ ] Do NOT click anything else on the page that opened
- [ ] Take a photo of your screen with your phone, document what you saw
- [ ] Tell your manager or the most senior person (in terms of position or hierarchy) available right now
- [ ] Do not use the same device to check if "anything happened", in fact; don't touch it. What's   done is done

**Do this in the next hour:**
- [ ] From a DIFFERENT device, change the password of any account 
      you were logged into on the affected device
- [ ] If you use the same password elsewhere, change those too
- [ ] Check if any accounts have new login activity you don't recognise
      (Gmail → Security → Recent activity)
- [ ] Contact your IT vendor and describe exactly what happened

**What not to do:**
- Do not pay any "fee" or "fine" a pop-up tells you to pay
- Do not call any phone number that appeared on screen
- Do not tell yourself "it was probably nothing" and move on
---

## "I gave my password to a website"

**Do this immediately— faster is better:**

- [ ] Go to the real website (type the URL yourself, do not click any link) 
      and change your password right now
- [ ] Enable two-factor authentication (2FA) if you haven't already
- [ ] Check the account's login history for unrecognised sessions 
      and log out all other sessions
- [ ] If it was your Singpass password:
      Call Singpass helpdesk immediately: **6335-3533**
- [ ] If it was your work email:
      Tell your manager and IT vendor within the hour— 
      your entire contact list may now be at risk of receiving 
      phishing emails that appear to come from you, do this as soon as possible.
- [ ] If it was your internet banking password:
      Call your bank's fraud hotline immediately and ask them to 
      freeze the account.

**What not to do:**
- Do not wait to see if anything bad happens, it will happen if you do nothing.
- Do not reuse the compromised password anywhere else
- Do not assume 2FA makes you completely safe— 
  some attacks can intercept OTPs too
---

## "Money has left our account"

**This is a financial emergency. Move fast.**

- [ ] Call your bank's fraud hotline immediately and ask them to 
      freeze the account— every minute matters
- [ ] Do not transfer money to any "safe account" someone tells you to— 
      this is a common follow-up scam
- [ ] File a police report at police.gov.sg/iwitness or call **999**
- [ ] Call the Anti-Scam Helpline: **1800-722-6688** (24 hours)
- [ ] Preserve all evidence— emails, screenshots, transaction records
- [ ] Notify your board or leadership immediately— 
      if donor or client funds are involved, they need to know

**What not to do:**
- Do not pay a "recovery fee" to anyone offering to get your money back
- Do not engage further with whoever contacted you
- Do not clean up or delete any communications with the scammer

---

## "We may have leaked personal data"

Under PDPA, if personal data has been or is likely to have been accessed, 
collected, used or disclosed without authorisation, you may be required 
to notify the PDPC and affected individuals.

- [ ] Document what data may have been exposed 
      (whose data, what type, how much)
- [ ] Identify when the breach likely occurred
- [ ] Consult a lawyer or DPO (Data Protection Officer) immediately
- [ ] If the breach is significant, notify PDPC within **3 calendar days**
      of becoming aware: pdpc.gov.sg/Overview-of-PDPA/The-Legislation/
      Personal-Data-Protection-Act/Data-Breach-Notification
- [ ] Notify affected individuals if the breach is likely to result in 
     harm to them

**What counts as harm:**
physical, psychological, financial harm, damage to reputation,
loss of employment or even identity theft

## What To Tell People When You Report

When you call any of the above, have this information ready:

- What happened, in plain language
- When you first noticed it
- What device was affected (laptop, phone, tablet)
- What accounts were logged in on that device
- Whether any money moved or any data was accessed
- What you've already done in response

You don't need to know technical details. 
Just describe what you saw and what you did.
