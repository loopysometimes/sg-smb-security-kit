# Want your office to NOT get hacked?
### A PDPA Checklist for Small Singapore Organisations!

This checklist is for small businesses that handle personal data but don't have an IT team. (you might wanna get one...)

*This checklist was prepared as a practical guide for small  organisations. **It is not legal advice**. For specific PDPA compliance questions, consult the PDPC's advisory guidelines or a qualified data protection officer.*

*I'm not a lawyer in any way, please **do not take this as legal advice**, it's just a project I thought was neat.*

PDPA (Personal Data Protection Act) doesn't require you to be a tech expert, all it asks of is for you to be responsible.

This checklist tells you exactly what that looks like in practice! Fix what you can before it's too late!
---

## 1. Do you know what personal data you're holding?

Personal data is anything that identifies a person like names, NRICs, phone 
numbers, email addresses, photos, medical information, financial details.

**Check these:**
- [ ] We have a list (even a simple spreadsheet) of what personal data we collect
- [ ] We know why we collected it and what we use it for
- [ ] We don't collect data we don't actually need
- [ ] We have a way for people to ask what data we hold about them

**What good looks like:**
A simple Google Sheet listing type of data, why you have it, where it's 
stored, who can access it. That's really it!

**Red flag:**
If you genuinely don't know what personal data your office holds, that'd be a little concerning but it's fixable!
---

## 2. Who has access to what?

Not everyone in your office needs access to everything. A volunteer shouldn't 
be able to see donor financial records. An intern shouldn't have access to 
your full client database.

**Check these:**
- [ ] We have different access levels for different staff roles
- [ ] We remove access immediately when someone leaves the organisation
- [ ] Shared passwords are not used for accounts containing personal data
- [ ] We know who has admin access to our key systems

**What good looks like:**
Your CRM or database has individual logins per person. When a staff member 
leaves, their account is disabled the same day and not "whenever we get around 
to it"

**Red flag:**
If your whole office shares one login for your donor database or client 
management system, this one's VERY concerning. One shared password means one 
phished staff member exposes everything so make this a high priority!
---

## 3. Are your shared drives and storage protected?

Some small offices store personal data in Google Drive, Dropbox, or a shared 
server folder. These are convenient but can easily be misconfigured to expose 
data publicly or to people who shouldn't see it since there's only one access point!

**Check these:**
- [ ] Folders containing personal data are not set to "anyone with the link"
- [ ] We regularly review who has access to shared drives
- [ ] Personal data is not stored in personal Gmail or personal Dropbox accounts
- [ ] Laptops containing personal data have screen lock enabled
- [ ] We know what happens to personal data if a staff laptop is lost or stolen

**What good looks like:**
Your Google Drive folders containing client or donor data are set to 
"specific people only." You do a quick access audit every few days or months and also remove ex-staff, old volunteers, former board members; essentially just anyone who should not have access to this sensitive information!

**Red flag:**
Search your Google Drive for "NRIC", "passport", or "IC number." 
If files come up that are shared publicly or with people who no longer 
work with you, fix that immediately.
---

## 4. How long are you keeping personal data?

Under PDPA, you can only keep personal data for as long as you have a 
legitimate reason to. Keeping donor records from 2015 for someone who 
asked to be removed is a breach. So is keeping job applicant CVs 
indefinitely "just in case."

**Check these:**
- [ ] We have a rough policy for how long we keep different types of data
- [ ] We delete or anonymise data we no longer need
- [ ] We have a process for when someone asks us to delete their data
- [ ] Old physical documents with personal data are shredded, not just binned

**What good looks like:**
A simple rule like: "We keep client records for 3 years after their last 
interaction, then delete them." Written down somewhere. Followed consistently.

**Red flag:**
If you have spreadsheets or filing cabinets full of data from people you 
haven't been in contact with for years and no policy for dealing with it,
that's a liability!
---

## 5. Do your staff know what a phishing email looks like?

The most sophisticated data protection system in the world fails if one 
staff member clicks a malicious link. Human error is the number one cause 
of data breaches in small organisations.

**Check these:**
- [ ] Staff know not to click links in unexpected emails, even from known senders
- [ ] Staff know that government agencies (IRAS, CPF, Singpass) never ask for 
      passwords or other sensitive information by email
- [ ] Staff know who to tell if they think they've clicked something suspicious
- [ ] We've talked about phishing as a team at least once in the last year

**What good looks like:**
A team conversation where you show one example of a real Singapore 
phishing email and ask "what are the red flags?".

**Common Singapore phishing red flags to share with your team:**
- Fake Singpass "your account will be suspended/deleted" emails
- Fake IRAS tax refund notifications asking you to "verify your bank details"
- Fake SingPost delivery notices asking for a small redelivery fee
- Any email creating urgency + asking you to click a link + asking for credentials

## If you're not sure where to start

**Do these three things! :**

1. Search your shared drives for files containing NRICs or passport numbers. 
   Check who has access. Fix any that are set to public.

2. Check that ex-staff no longer have access to your systems. 
   Remove any that do.

3. Forward one example of a Singapore phishing email to your team 
   and ask them to spot the red flags. 
   (CSA's SingCERT website has real examples.)

## Where to get help

**PDPC (Personal Data Protection Commission)**
The PDPC enforces Singapore's Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) while also acting as the regulator ensuring that businesses handle, store, collect, and transfer individuals' personal data responsibly. they also provide free guides for small organisations!

https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/ 

**CSA SingCERT**
SingCERT operates under the Cyber Security Agency of Singapore (CSA). It serves as the national emergency responder for cyber threats affecting businesses, infrastructure, and the general public.Reports, while also providing real phishing examples.

https://www.csa.gov.sg/singcert

**ScamShield**
Developed by Open Government Products (OGP) in collaboration with the Singapore Police Force (SPF) and the National Crime Prevention Council (NCPC), ScamShield is a public-facing defense, designed to filter and block scam attempts. Essentially it's a free app that blocks known scam calls and SMSes.

https://www.scamshield.gov.sg
